## Reading

This section is written by hand and preserved when the report is regenerated.

The measurement trips the gate: without correction, there are residuals above 0.5" in the Moon
up to the 1900s decade and again from 2019 on, and in all bodies from
2020 on. The "Per-chart fit" table shows that this has a single,
reproducible cause.

### Measured facts

- Fitting, in each of the 290 charts, the differences from the Sun to Saturn to the
  model `a + speed × dt`, the largest residual left is 0.0004".
- `a` stays between 0.1380" and 0.1385" in every chart, from 1801 to 2048.
- `dt` depends only on the date: near −4 s at the start of the 19th century, rising
  to zero around 1920–1960, zero from 1960 to about 2015, and growing
  to about 40 s in 2048.
- The fit is done by `fitChart`, has a test that runs over all the
  reference sets, and the table is regenerated by the measurement command.
- The same pair (`a`, `dt`) of each chart also explains Uranus, Neptune and
  Pluto; this was checked in the code review, it is not in the table.
- The true node, with `a` removed, differs by up to about 0.5", with the sign
  varying from decade to decade and no relation to `dt`.
- The ten charts with the largest raw residual are all from 2041 to 2046, of the
  random or recent kind; none is a station or boundary case.

### Interpretation (hypotheses, not verified in the executable)

- **Delta T.** A time offset that depends only on the date is what a
  different Delta T table produces: zero where both use the same observed
  values, a few seconds in the 19th century, tens of seconds in the
  future, where each extrapolates in its own way. It is the adopted explanation.
- **Ayanamsha.** Resolved later, by the extraction with the other ayanamshas:
  the 0.138" offset belongs to Lahiri. Yukteshwar and Bhasin have no
  offset, and Raman, Fagan and Krishnamurti have different constants. See
  `docs/spec/ayanamsha.md`.
- **Delta T.** Confirmed later: PL9's Delta T is that of Swiss Ephemeris
  2.00.00, to within 0.008 s over the 290 charts. See `docs/spec/delta-t.md`.
- **Origin.** The simplest explanation for both facts is that PL9's own
  ephemeris was generated with an earlier version of the Swiss
  Ephemeris, with that version's Delta T and Lahiri.

### Answers to the plan's questions

1. **Is the common offset constant?** Yes, 0.138", once the
   time effect is separated. The "Median offset" column of the "By decade" table
   varies because it uses a simple median that includes the Sun and mixes the two
   effects; the residuals of Mars, Jupiter and Saturn in that table are
   inflated for the same reason.
2. **Do the slow planets' residuals stay below 0.5"?** Without correcting the
   time, not after 2020. Corrected, they stay below 0.001".
3. **Does the Moon's residual grow at either end?** At both: about 2.8" in
   1800–1830 and up to 25" in 2040–2048. It is `dt` multiplied by the Moon's
   speed.
4. **Node.** Up to about 0.5", without following `dt`. The node's model is
   different and remains to be investigated.
5. **Worst charts.** See "Measured facts".

### Consequence for the 0.5" criterion

The criterion is reachable with margin from the Sun to Pluto if `core` reproduces
PL9's `dt` as a function of the date and adds 0.138". Still to be determined, in plan 2B:
the function `dt(date)` over the whole range, the nature of the 0.138" offset,
and the model of the true node. The Lagna and the derived points have not yet
been measured.

### Coverage of the reference set

- The design calls for constructed cases of "Moon near the nodes" and of birth
  close to sunrise and sunset. Neither is in this set; both
  are left for plan 2B.
- The boundary pairs were placed 1.5" from the boundary according to the
  Swiss Ephemeris. Since PL9's Moon differs from it by up to 2.9" before 1900
  and by up to 25" after 2020, a Moon pair in those epochs may fall entirely
  on one side only in PL9. The current pairs are on opposite sides in
  PL9 too, and there is a test for that; new pairs must be placed with `dt`
  applied.
- None of the current boundary pairs catches a retrograde body.
